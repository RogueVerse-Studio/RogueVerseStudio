# Beyond My Hero Academia: Is Horror Kōhei Horikoshi’s Next Era?

**Deck:** The creator of one of modern shōnen manga’s biggest superhero stories is returning to *Weekly Shōnen Jump* with a 61-page horror one-shot. Here is what has actually been announced—and why the genre change may fit him better than the headline suggests.

**Section:** AniManga Updates  
**Content type:** Deep feature  
**Byline:** RogueVerse Studio  
**Status:** Pre-publication information current as of July 30, 2026

Kōhei Horikoshi is returning to *Weekly Shōnen Jump*, but he is not bringing another class of superheroes with him.

His newly announced manga, **笑わないでよ しじまさん**—romanized as *Warawanaide yo, Shijima-san* and informally translated as *Don’t Laugh, Shijima-san* or *Please Don’t Laugh, Shijima-san*—is being described as a **“boy-meets-girl horror”** story.

That alone is enough to make this one of the most interesting manga announcements of the year. Horikoshi spent a decade building *My Hero Academia* into a global franchise defined by colorful powers, heroic ideals and explosive battles. His first completely new published story after that era will apparently move into something quieter, stranger and much darker.

But before the hype train leaves the station wearing a villain cape, one important correction needs to be made.

## This is a one-shot, not a confirmed new series

The official announcement is for a **61-page one-shot** scheduled to appear in the combined Issue 37/38 of *Weekly Shōnen Jump*, which goes on sale in Japan on **August 10, 2026**. The story will also receive center-color treatment. Japanese reports describe the release as “center color, 61 pages,” which means a 61-page story with featured color pages—not necessarily a fully colored 61-page manga.

The wording matters because several English-language headlines have made the announcement sound like Horikoshi is already launching his next long-running serialization. He is not—at least not based on anything Shueisha or Jump has announced.

No ongoing series has been confirmed. No chapter two has been announced. No collected volume, anime adaptation or connection to the *My Hero Academia* universe has been revealed. There is not even an official English title yet.

What we have is one large, self-contained story and one teaser illustration showing a male protagonist beside a mysterious heroine.

That is still a major return. It is simply a different kind of return than the louder headlines suggest. [ORICON NEWS](https://www.oricon.co.jp/news/2470553/full/) and [Comic Natalie](https://natalie.mu/comic/news/682768) both describe the project as a one-shot, while the [official *My Hero Academia* account](https://x.com/heroaca_info/status/2082326946667708732) confirms the August 10 publication date and “boy-meets-girl horror” description.

## Why the horror direction makes sense

At first glance, moving from superhero shōnen to horror looks like a sharp turn. Look more closely at Horikoshi’s artwork, though, and the road has been there the entire time.

*My Hero Academia* was bright on the surface, but some of its strongest images were built from horror. Horikoshi repeatedly used distorted anatomy, damaged bodies, invasive transformations, heavy shadow and expressions that pushed characters past normal human limits. His villains did not just look dangerous; many of them looked physically wrong in ways designed to make the reader uncomfortable.

That visual language showed up in decaying skin, multiplying hands, broken smiles, unnatural growth and bodies changing under the pressure of powers they could barely contain. Even when the story remained hopeful, Horikoshi understood how to make a page feel unsafe.

Horror could give him room to stop pulling that instinct back.

In a superhero manga, disturbing imagery usually serves the action, the villain or the escalation of a battle. In a horror story, the discomfort can become the point. A shadow does not need to hide an opponent waiting for the next fight. It can hide something the reader may never fully understand. A strange smile does not have to introduce a superpower. It can be frightening simply because somebody should not be smiling at all.

The announced “boy-meets-girl” framework makes that even more intriguing. That phrase usually suggests connection, romance or coming-of-age discovery. Adding horror to it creates an immediate question: is the girl somebody the protagonist needs to save, somebody he should fear—or both?

Jump has not released enough story information to answer that. That mystery is part of the appeal.

## The title may be doing double duty

The most direct translation of *Warawanaide yo, Shijima-san* is something close to “Don’t Laugh, Shijima-san” or “Please Don’t Laugh, Shijima-san.” Shijima appears to be the heroine’s name, but the word *shijima* can also evoke silence or deep stillness in Japanese.

Because the title writes Shijima phonetically rather than using kanji that would settle the meaning, readers may be meant to hear both the name and the feeling of silence underneath it.

That does not give us a secret synopsis. It is only a possible layer of wordplay. Still, “do not laugh,” a mysterious girl and an atmosphere of silence are a strong horror combination before the first page has even arrived.

The teaser image supports that restrained mood. It does not sell the story through a giant creature, graphic violence or an obvious villain. The unease comes from the relationship between two ordinary-looking figures and from whatever is not being explained yet. That suggests the one-shot may lean toward psychological or supernatural tension rather than pure action-horror, although that remains speculation until publication.

## Is this the project Horikoshi mentioned in 2024?

There is another reason fans are watching this one-shot closely.

After *My Hero Academia* ended in August 2024, Horikoshi said he intended to create a new manga title as soon as he could. He explained that he had considered dividing the work and focusing only on the artwork, but his editor encouraged him to continue handling the full creation process. At the time, Horikoshi said he was slowly working from the storyboard stage while still dealing with remaining *My Hero Academia* events and commitments. [ORICON reported those comments in December 2024](https://www.oricon.co.jp/news/2357350/full/).

Could *Shijima-san* be that project?

Possibly. A 61-page one-shot is substantial, and the timing makes the connection tempting. But there is no confirmation that this is the same “new title” Horikoshi discussed, and it would be irresponsible to present the connection as fact.

The one-shot could be a finished version of that early work. It could also be an experiment created later, a standalone story Horikoshi wanted to tell or a pilot that Jump may evaluate for future expansion.

Manga history gives fans a reason to keep the pilot possibility in mind. Horikoshi’s 2008 one-shot *My Hero* contained early creative DNA that eventually developed into *My Hero Academia*. That does not mean every one-shot is secretly a series proposal. It does mean a short work can become a laboratory where a creator tests a new tone, character dynamic or world before deciding whether to go further.

## What this return could tell us

The biggest question may not be whether *Shijima-san* becomes a series. It may be what the story reveals about Horikoshi after ten years of drawing the same universe on a punishing weekly schedule.

Long-running manga creators are often defined by the title that made them famous. Audiences naturally ask for sequels, spin-offs or something that reproduces the same feeling. But a creator coming out of a decade-long serialization may want the exact opposite: a smaller cast, a different rhythm and a story that does not have to carry the weight of an existing franchise.

A one-shot is perfect for that kind of reset. Sixty-one pages are enough to build atmosphere, introduce a relationship and land a real ending. At the same time, the story does not have to establish years of power systems, rivalries and future arcs. Horikoshi can make one complete statement, see how readers respond and decide what comes next without promising another marathon.

That freedom may be more important than the genre label.

If the horror succeeds, it could prove that the qualities readers responded to in *My Hero Academia* were never limited to superheroes. Horikoshi’s real strengths include expressive faces, emotional vulnerability, instantly readable silhouettes, cinematic page composition and the ability to make a character’s inner damage visible on the outside. Every one of those tools can work in horror.

Maybe even better.

## What remains unknown

As of July 30, the following details have not been officially announced:

- A full plot synopsis
- The characters’ names beyond the likely use of Shijima-san
- Whether the horror is supernatural, psychological, romantic or body horror
- An ongoing serialization
- An official English title
- A simultaneous English release through VIZ or MANGA Plus
- Any connection to *My Hero Academia*
- A collected edition or adaptation

Those blanks are important. They are also why this announcement has created so much curiosity from so little material.

The accurate headline is not “Horikoshi launches his next major series.” It is this: **the creator of *My Hero Academia* is returning with a long horror one-shot that may show us what his art looks like without superheroes holding the darkness back.**

That is already enough.

The cape is off. The hallway lights are flickering. On August 10, we find out what is standing at the other end.
