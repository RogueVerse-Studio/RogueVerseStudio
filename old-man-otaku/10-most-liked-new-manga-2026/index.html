10 of the Most-Liked Newer Manga of 2026

A verified midyear list built from real 2026 award attention and reader-backed nomination data, with one important correction: these are newer manga gaining major support in 2026, not necessarily series that all debuted this year.

10 MANGA READERS ARE BACKING IN 2026

REAL TITLES • REAL AWARD SIGNALS • NO INVENTED RANKINGS

Correction from the earlier version: RogueVerse previously published an unreliable draft of this feature that mixed unsupported titles and treated “new manga of 2026” too loosely. That version was removed. This rebuild uses only titles and popularity signals we could verify through current 2026 award and publisher reporting.

How this list was built

There is no single global scoreboard for “most liked manga.” Rather than pretend one exists, RogueVerse used two concrete 2026 signals. First, Manga Taisho 2026, whose selection process is driven largely by bookstore staff and manga enthusiasts choosing books they most want to recommend. Second, the Next Manga Awards 2026, a user-participation award that drew 7,952 entries before narrowing the field to 100 nominees.

Manga Taisho's official rules say its 2026 field was drawn from works that had new collected volumes published during 2025 and had no more than eight volumes, which means the award is specifically designed to surface relatively young series rather than long-established giants. The Next Manga Awards describes itself as a reader-participation award focused on what is likely to break out next. Its 2026 final results are not due until September 16, so nomination alone is a popularity signal, not a final rank.

Because the evidence does not support a precise one-through-ten popularity order, this article is presented as 10 verified standouts, not a fake numerical ranking.

Books Aplenty (Hon Nara Uru Hodo)

Verified signal: Manga Taisho 2026 winner

Ao Kojima's human drama about a secondhand bookstore and the people whose lives cross there won the Manga Taisho 2026 grand prize. That is the strongest verified recommendation signal on this list. Manga Taisho is chosen by people deeply embedded in manga culture, especially bookstore staff, and the award describes its mission as identifying the manga readers most want to recommend right now.

It is also useful evidence that 2026's most celebrated newer manga are not limited to action and fantasy. A quiet story about books, memory, and human connection can still cut through a market flooded with high-concept hooks.

Ichi the Witch

Verified signal: Manga Taisho 2026 finalist

Written by Osamu Nishi and illustrated by Shiro Usazaki, Ichi the Witch appeared among Manga Taisho's 2026 finalists. Shueisha's July 2026 release list also shows the series already reaching volume 9, evidence of sustained serialization momentum rather than a one-week flash of hype.

Its appeal is easy to understand: an immediately readable fantasy premise, strong character acting, and art that gives magical action a clean visual identity. It is one of the clearest examples of a young Jump property successfully building recognition beyond weekly readers.

Thank You Pitch (San-kyuu Pitch)

Verified signal: Manga Taisho 2026 finalist

Yuu Sumiyoshi's baseball manga also made the Manga Taisho finalist field. Sports manga live or die on character chemistry and the ability to turn tactical decisions into drama, and a Manga Taisho nomination is meaningful evidence that the series is connecting beyond a narrow baseball audience.

Its inclusion also helps keep this list honest. “Popular manga” in Japan is much wider than the battle-shonen bubble that dominates international conversation.

Rojiura no Fujii (Fujii in the Street Corner)

Verified signal: Manga Taisho 2026 finalist

Tsuneo Nabekura's Rojiura no Fujii was another Manga Taisho finalist. It stands out because its appeal is rooted in everyday observation rather than spectacle. The manga's presence in the final field indicates strong recommendation energy among selectors who had hundreds of eligible series to choose from.

That kind of support matters for RogueVerse's definition of “liked.” Viral attention is one kind of popularity. The slower process of readers handing a book to someone and saying, “You need to read this,” is another.

RIOT

Verified signal: Manga Taisho 2026 finalist

Yuta Tsukada's RIOT earned a place among the Manga Taisho 2026 finalists. The nomination is the important fact here. We are not assigning it a made-up readership number or pretending to know its national sales position. What we can say is that it survived a recommendation-driven selection process designed to identify the most compelling young manga of the moment.

The Man-Eating Mansion and Its Landlord (Hitokui Mansion to Ooya no Maison)

Verified signal: Manga Taisho 2026 finalist

Created by Akima and Sora Tanaka, this title also reached the Manga Taisho final field. Its high-concept premise makes it exactly the kind of series that can travel through recommendation culture: strange enough to pitch quickly, but grounded enough in character relationships to keep readers around.

My Little Sister Knows (Imouto wa Shitteiru)

Verified signal: Manga Taisho 2026 finalist

Mari Gangimari's Imouto wa Shitteiru is another official 2026 Manga Taisho finalist. Its selection reinforces a recurring pattern in this year's field: relationship-driven manga and human-scale comedy are competing successfully with more obviously cinematic genres.

Okaeri Suiheisen

Verified signal: Manga Taisho 2026 finalist + Next Manga Awards 2026 nominee

Daigo Watanabe's Okaeri Suiheisen has one of the strongest cross-award signals in the rebuild. It appears in Manga Taisho's finalist field and also in the Next Manga Awards 2026 web-manga nominations. When two different selection systems, one recommendation-driven and one explicitly user-participatory, surface the same title, that is a much better popularity indicator than an unsourced social-media impression.

2nd-Year Class B: Hero Destroyers (2-nen B-gumi Yuusha Destroyers)

Verified signal: 2026 debut + Next Manga Awards 2026 nominee

This is the cleanest true 2026 debut on the list. Hideaki Sorachi, creator of Gintama, launched the comedy in Weekly Shonen Jump on April 20, 2026, his first new serialization in roughly seven years. By June, it had already been named among the 40 print-category nominees for the Next Manga Awards 2026.

That does not tell us where it will place in September, but it does verify both parts of the story that matter: it is genuinely new in 2026, and it attracted enough reader nomination support to reach a national award field.

Alien Headbutt

Verified signal: Next Manga Awards 2026 nominee + Shueisha volume release

Akira Inui's Alien Headbutt is another title we can verify without inflating the evidence. It was selected for the 2026 Next Manga Awards print category, and Shueisha's official July release schedule lists volumes 1 and 2. That combination makes it a legitimate early-run series to watch right now.

The point is not to declare it a guaranteed breakout before the votes are counted. The point is that it has already cleared two useful thresholds: a formal collected-volume release and enough reader interest to make a competitive national nomination list.

What changed from the original RogueVerse list?

The previous draft tried to force a neat top ten before the evidence justified one. That created two problems: several titles were not properly verified, and established manga were mixed with actual 2026 newcomers under the same “new” label.

This rebuild uses a narrower claim. These are newer manga receiving documented support in 2026. Some began before January. Some debuted this year. All ten have a concrete, traceable 2026 signal.

We also deliberately did not include Kagurabachi simply because it is popular. It began in 2023 and is already far enough into its run that calling it “new manga of 2026” would repeat the same category mistake. The same logic applies to other established hits.

What to watch next

The biggest missing piece is the final result of the Next Manga Awards 2026. The official site says results will be announced on September 16, 2026. When that happens, RogueVerse can update this feature with actual user-vote placements instead of nomination-level signals.

That future update will give us a much firmer basis for a true ranked list. Until then, pretending we know the exact ten “most liked” titles would be editorial theater. This version chooses evidence over symmetry.
