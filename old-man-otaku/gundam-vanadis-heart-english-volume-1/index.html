Before Suletta Mercury: Vanadis Heart Opens a Missing Chapter of The Witch from Mercury

The first English volume does more than revisit Ad Stella. It explores what happened to GUND technology and the people connected to Vanadis while the wound was still fresh.

For English-speaking Gundam readers, one of the most interesting corners of the Ad Stella timeline has finally received a major new doorway. Mobile Suit Gundam: The Witch from Mercury—Vanadis Heart Volume 1 releases in English through Yen Press on August 25, 2026.

Calling Vanadis Heart simply a spin-off undersells what it does. The manga is set in A.S. 106, five years after the Vanadis Incident, placing its story between the tragedy that establishes The Witch from Mercury’s world and the era eventually inhabited by Suletta Mercury.

Instead of following another student through Asticassia, Vanadis Heart returns to Earth and asks what happened to GUND technology, Gundams and the people connected to Vanadis after the world decided they should disappear.

The Timeline Matters

The Vanadis Incident is one of the foundational events behind The Witch from Mercury. In its aftermath, Gundams are forbidden and the legacy of GUND technology becomes inseparable from the idea that these machines are a dangerous “curse.”

Vanadis Heart takes place while that wound is still relatively fresh. The political, technological and human consequences are still unfolding. That makes the manga a bridge rather than a conventional side adventure. It explores how Ad Stella moved from the Vanadis catastrophe toward the status quo eventually encountered in the television series.

Meet Kiyu Lavot and Vilda Miren

The story follows Kiyu Lavot, a young assistant traveling across Earth with Vilda Miren. Vilda previously researched GUND medical technology as a doctor. Together they travel using a massive transport trailer.

What they are carrying makes the journey considerably more dangerous: a Gundam, specifically Gundam Lfrith Jiu.

The Earth setting immediately separates the manga from the school and corporate environments that dominate much of The Witch from Mercury. Yen Press describes Kiyu and Vilda as crossing a planet marked by violent conflict and economic inequality, putting them close to the human consequences of Ad Stella’s political systems.

Gundam Lfrith Jiu and the Tiger Hand

The XGF-01[II3] Gundam Lfrith Jiu is piloted by Kiyu and carries the visual DNA expected from an Ad Stella Gundam. Its defining feature is an enormous composite right-arm weapon known as the Tiger Hand.

According to the official Gundam profile, the weapon uses the right arm of another machine. It gives Lfrith Jiu a striking asymmetrical silhouette, but its narrative significance goes beyond appearance. The machine keeps the story physically connected to the forbidden Gundam lineage. Gundams have not vanished. Their consequences have simply been pushed into places the wider world would rather not look.

The GUND Question Never Went Away

This is where Vanadis Heart becomes especially interesting for viewers drawn to the underlying ideas of The Witch from Mercury. GUND was never merely technology that makes Gundams function. Its roots are medical, tied to helping human bodies overcome limitations created by life beyond Earth.

That ideal is fundamental to the Vanadis tragedy. A technology intended to extend human possibility becomes associated with machines capable of destroying human life.

Vilda’s journey sits directly inside that contradiction. She is not merely transporting a secret robot. She is carrying a physical reminder of an argument the world attempted to settle through prohibition: can GUND still fulfill the promise that inspired it, or did the creation of Gundams permanently corrupt that dream?

Earth Changes the View

The television series shows the enormous divide between Earthians and Spacians, especially once its story expands beyond Asticassia. Vanadis Heart gets to live inside that divide rather than visit it temporarily.

That matters because Gundam has always been strongest when its giant machines reveal something about the people forced to live beneath them. A story built around travel across a conflict-scarred Earth gives Vanadis Heart room to explore the consequences of Ad Stella from ground level.

Creative Continuity With The Witch from Mercury

The manga is illustrated by Chika Tojo, with scenario by Kou Yoneyama and planning cooperation by HISADAKE. MOGUMO is credited with original character-design concepts, while the mechanical-design team includes Kanetake Ebikawa, Ippei Gyoubu and Kenji Teraoka.

That creative continuity helps the spin-off feel like an expansion of Ad Stella rather than an unrelated manga borrowing the Gundam name. The series began in Monthly Gundam Ace in 2023 and has continued beyond the material English readers are receiving in this first volume.

What English Volume 1 Includes

Yen Press lists the paperback at 180 pages, with a digital edition also available. English credits include Chika Tojo for manga art, Kou Yoneyama for scenario, HISADAKE for planning cooperation, Hajime Yatate and Yoshiyuki Tomino for original creator credits, Stephen Paul for translation and Brandon Bovia for lettering.

The paperback is listed at $13.00 USD / $17.00 CAD, while the digital edition is $6.99 USD / $8.99 CAD. Yen Press rates the title Teen and describes it as the first official spin-off manga from The Witch from Mercury.

Do You Need to Watch The Witch from Mercury First?

Technically, Vanadis Heart begins earlier in the chronology. But chronological order is not automatically the strongest first-time experience.

A newcomer can understand the basic premise of Kiyu, Vilda and the forbidden Gundam they are transporting. The larger significance becomes clearer if you already understand the Vanadis Incident, why Gundams were prohibited, what GUND technology was intended to accomplish, the Earthian-Spacian divide and why the word “witch” carries political weight in this universe.

For newcomers, a strong route is The Witch from Mercury Prologue → television anime → Vanadis Heart. For established fans, the A.S. 106 placement is precisely what makes the manga attractive.

Why This English Release Matters

The Witch from Mercury gave a large new audience a self-contained entry into Gundam while still carrying the franchise’s recurring interests in political power, war, corporate control, technology, inequality and young people inheriting conflicts created before they were born.

Vanadis Heart gives that audience somewhere else to go inside the same continuity without simply offering “more Suletta.” It expands the world sideways: different protagonists, different Gundam, different environment, different moment in history, same central wound.

That is what a good spin-off can do. Instead of shrinking a universe by making every important event revolve around the original cast, it makes that universe feel larger by showing that important lives and conflicts were happening elsewhere.

RogueVerse Take

The English debut of Vanadis Heart is more than another manga volume arriving on shelves. It is an opportunity for Witch from Mercury viewers to return to Ad Stella from a different direction.

Suletta’s story showed us a world living with the legacy of Gundams. Kiyu and Vilda’s story moves closer to the period when that legacy was still being written.

At the center sits Gundam Lfrith Jiu: forbidden technology traveling across a damaged Earth while its companions confront whether GUND represents humanity’s future or a curse humanity was right to fear.

For fans who finished The Witch from Mercury wanting more of its world rather than simply more of its protagonists, Vanadis Heart may be the most interesting place to look next.
