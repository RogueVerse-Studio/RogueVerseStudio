Blue Box Season 2 Sets October 4 Premiere as New Trailer Reveals Its Next Emotional Collision

A new studio, a new director and a much more complicated winter are waiting for Taiki and Chinatsu.

BLUE BOX
SEASON 2

Blue Box is officially stepping back onto the court this fall. The sequel begins its Japanese television broadcast on October 4, 2026, and its newly released main trailer and visual give fans their clearest look yet at the next stage of Taiki Inomata and Chinatsu Kano’s story.

The trailer previews aiko’s opening theme, “Anata no Hana no Iro,” while Elsewhere Kikou performs the ending theme, “blue in.” But the music and premiere date are only half the story. Season 2 also represents a substantial behind-the-scenes transition for one of Shonen Jump’s most recognizable recent romance adaptations.

Blue Box is changing studios

The first season was animated by Telecom Animation Film with Yuichiro Yano directing. For Season 2, animation production moves to Electric Circus, while Daisuke Sakō takes over as director.

That is not a minor credits shuffle. A studio and director change can alter episode pacing, shot composition, character acting, sports animation and the rhythm of quieter emotional scenes. That matters especially for Blue Box, whose appeal depends as much on tiny pauses, expressions and awkward moments as it does on badminton or basketball.

Several important creative anchors remain. Yuko Kakihara returns for series composition and scripts, Miho Tanino returns as character designer and chief animation director, and Takashi Ohmama remains on music. That continuity gives the sequel a bridge back to what already worked.

Season 2 at a glance

  • Japanese TV premiere: October 4, 2026
  • Animation studio: Electric Circus
  • Director: Daisuke Sakō
  • Series composition: Yuko Kakihara
  • Character design: Miho Tanino
  • Opening: “Anata no Hana no Iro” by aiko
  • Ending: “blue in” by Elsewhere Kikou

The new trailer puts Chinatsu under pressure

The footage signals that Season 2 will not simply resume the romantic momentum between Taiki and Chinatsu. A major part of the material centers on Yumeka Kido, Chinatsu’s former basketball teammate, pulling unresolved history back into the present.

That creates the kind of conflict Blue Box handles particularly well: personal tension without needing a conventional villain. Season 2 appears ready to ask what happens when the people around Chinatsu force her to confront parts of herself that Taiki cannot simply solve for her.

The manga has already reached its ending

Season 2 also arrives in an unusual position because Kouji Miura’s manga concluded with Chapter 250 in July 2026. The anime is therefore adapting a story whose complete destination now exists rather than following an unfinished serialization.

For viewers, that potentially changes the relationship with the adaptation. Production choices can eventually be measured against a completed roadmap, and the anime still has substantial material available if it continues beyond this season.

Same heart, different machinery

The recognizable voice cast and several core creative staff remain while the director and animation studio change. That makes this less like a reboot and more like a carefully controlled handoff.

The question is whether Electric Circus and Sakō can preserve the restraint that made Season 1 work while giving the sequel its own visual rhythm. With Yumeka’s arrival, Chinatsu’s past becoming more important and Taiki and Chinatsu continuing to move closer, the story itself is not standing still either.

RogueVerse Take

Blue Box Season 2 is becoming one of the more interesting sequels of Fall 2026 precisely because its biggest changes are happening behind the camera. Switching both director and studio can be risky for a series built on subtle character acting, but retaining Kakihara, Tanino and other important staff members gives the sequel meaningful continuity.

The new trailer also suggests the emotional temperature is rising. Yumeka pulls Chinatsu’s history into the foreground while the romance, sports ambitions and awkward emotional machinery that define Blue Box keep moving.

Season 2 does not look like Blue Box simply reopening the same box. Someone shook it first.

Blue Box Season 2 premieres October 4, 2026 in Japan.
