Solo Leveling Returns November 5 With Three New Chapters—but It's Ragnarok, Not Anime Season 3

Sung Suho’s webtoon resumes with Chapters 69–71 and a new illustrator. No new television anime episodes are scheduled for that date.

By RogueVerse News · Old Man Otaku · November 3, 2026

What is confirmed: The Korean Solo Leveling: Ragnarok webtoon returns November 5 with three chapters. This is not a release of three anime episodes; an English Season 3 publication date remains unannounced.

Solo Leveling is coming back on Thursday, November 5, 2026, and fans have good reason to be excited. After nearly ten months away, Solo Leveling: Ragnarok is set to resume its Korean webtoon run with three installments released together: Episodes 69, 70, and 71. The launch takes place on KakaoPage, with new Korean chapters then planned for Thursdays.

That is the confirmed story behind headlines describing “three new Solo Leveling episodes.” It is also a distinction worth putting in the first paragraph rather than hiding at the bottom: these are webtoon episodes (chapters), not three anime episodes on Crunchyroll. The return concerns Ragnarok, the spin-off built around Sung Suho, son of original protagonist Sung Jinwoo. The TV anime does not return on November 5.

The news is substantial on its own. There is a long-awaited continuation, a new season, a new lead illustrator, and a story that has spent most of 2026 frozen at a cliffhanger. Here is what readers can actually expect—and what has not been announced.

The confirmed return: November 5, three webtoon episodes

According to the KakaoPage announcement described in reporting on October 30, Solo Leveling: Ragnarok Season 3 begins with Episodes 69–71 on November 5, 2026. The Korean-language release follows Season 2's conclusion at Episode 68 in January 2026. Reports say the regular release day after the opening batch will be Thursday.

There is one terminology trap here. Korean webtoon services commonly call individual installments “episodes.” In English-language anime coverage, however, “episode” usually means a televised installment. That ambiguity is the entire reason this headline is easy to misread. These three new episodes are chapters of a vertically scrolling comic.

For readers who follow Ragnarok through English-language platforms, the date needs an additional qualification: November 5 is the announced Korean-platform return, not a confirmed simultaneous English-language release. As of this article's publication, no official English return date for the new Season 3 chapters has been announced. Availability, time-zone display, and chapter labeling may differ by service. Check the individual platform rather than assuming all editions update at once.

Why Ragnarok has been away for so long

Season 2's finale appeared in January 2026, leaving readers waiting at Episode 68. The English edition's official Tapas page posted a hiatus notice on January 31, confirming that Season 2 had ended and that the series would return for Season 3. From the reader's perspective, that meant a remarkably long stretch without new chapters from a franchise otherwise known for momentum.

A major factor was a creative transition. Illustrator JIN, who worked on the earlier seasons, departed because of mandatory military service in South Korea. Replacing a lead artist is not the same as filling an empty staff position on a weekly comic. The visual continuity of the characters, the clarity of action scenes, and the speed of serialized production all depend on a shared understanding of the title's aesthetic. That makes the new illustrator one of this week's most important pieces of news.

Publisher D&C Webtoon revealed a new Season 3 cover on October 28 and identified Sanjimseung, previously associated with Ice Lord, as the incoming illustrator. The new visual places Suho back at the center of the campaign and marks the start of a different artistic chapter for the sequel. The original novel's author Daul and webtoon adaptation writer Dang Do remain part of the credit history reported with the announcement.

Changing artists can understandably concern an established readership, particularly for a series whose appeal depends so much on large, dynamic action compositions. But it would be unfair to declare the change a success or a failure from one cover alone. The real test will be several chapters of sequential storytelling: whether combat is legible, whether Suho's emotional expressions stay recognizable, and whether the art team can maintain a sustainable release rhythm.

Who is Sung Suho, and why does this sequel matter?

The original Solo Leveling tells the story of Sung Jinwoo, a hunter who begins as a notoriously weak participant in dangerous dungeon raids and acquires a mysterious ability to grow stronger. Ragnarok changes the central perspective. Sung Suho, Jinwoo's son, begins as a seemingly ordinary college student whose buried memories and inherited abilities start reawakening after a terrifying experience.

The official English series description frames Suho's journey around discovering his bloodline, dealing with the absence of his parents, and learning to master powers that place him at the center of another threat to Earth. He inherits a daunting legacy, but the appeal of the sequel is the possibility of discovering what he does differently from his father—not simply watching another character repeat Jinwoo's story.

There is a story-level reason the distinction matters. Jinwoo's transformation was about breaking the limits of a system that treated him as weak. Suho's predicament starts from inheritance: an extraordinary history already exists, and he has to discover what his place within it might be. That gives Ragnarok a naturally different question to explore. The audience already knows what a legendary hunter looks like. What does growing up in the shadow of that legend do to the next generation?

That is the more interesting version of “the son of the strongest hunter.” The family connection attracts readers; Suho's personal choices have to keep them there.

Spoiler note: Ragnarok operates after major developments in the original Solo Leveling story. Anime-only fans may encounter significant material beyond Season 2 if they jump directly into the sequel.

The triple release is a smart way to restart momentum

Dropping three chapters together is more than a generous welcome-back package. After a hiatus stretching from January into November, a single chapter might be consumed in minutes and leave readers feeling that the return barely happened. Three installments give the revived season space to re-establish its pace and invite the audience back into an ongoing conflict.

There are trade-offs. A triple launch raises immediate expectations for the new art team, while the planned weekly cadence creates pressure to deliver consistently afterward. The opening chapters will need to reorient casual readers without spending too long summarizing events that dedicated fans remember. For returning fans, that balance is everything: they want forward movement, but they also need to reconnect with the emotional and tactical context of the cliffhanger.

The most important thing to watch during November is not a single explosive splash page. It is whether the series can make readers confident that Ragnarok is back for the long run. Re-establishing trust after a long pause takes more than an announcement; it takes reliable storytelling over time.

Can English-language readers get the new chapters immediately?

No simultaneous English release has been confirmed. Readers can currently find the official English Solo Leveling: Ragnarok comic through Tapas and Tappytoon, but both services were still displaying hiatus-related information when this article was prepared. That means the Korean restart should not be advertised as a guaranteed November 5 English-language premiere.

This is an important difference for any fan about to search for “Episode 69 English” on release day. It is better to follow the official platforms' notices than to assume a scanlation or a repost is an authorized translation. The English edition has its own scheduling and rights arrangements; even when Korean publication resumes, localization does not necessarily follow instantly.

If you are catching up, look for Episode 68: S2 Finale on an official reader before continuing. The new material builds from where that episode left off, not from the end of the anime's second season.

What about Solo Leveling anime Season 3?

Here is the other critical clarification: Ragnarok Season 3 is not Solo Leveling anime Season 3. The latter is a different production, different release format, and—most importantly—does not have an announced premiere date tied to November 5.

The next major anime project publicly announced by Crunchyroll and Aniplex is the theatrical film *Solo Leveling: Beyond the System***. The companies revealed it at Anime Expo on **July 3, 2026, describing it as the next chapter of Sung Jinwoo's animated story following the events of the second TV season. A-1 Pictures is returning to animate the movie. As of November 3, the official anime news page and Crunchyroll's movie announcement do not provide a theatrical release date.

It is understandable that fans mentally connect the events: webtoon chapters are restarting; a film is in development; discussion of a third anime season continues. But these are three separate stories in an ongoing franchise news cycle. The existence of one should never be reported as a release date for another.

For that reason, RogueVerse is not presenting a 2026 TV return, an anime episode count, or a film premiere date as confirmed. All three would go beyond what the available official announcements establish.

The bigger picture: Solo Leveling is now more than one release schedule

One of the fascinating things about Solo Leveling in 2026 is how broadly its audience is distributed. There are fans who discovered Jinwoo through the original Korean webcomic, viewers who encountered the franchise through A-1 Pictures' television adaptation, readers who have followed Suho's sequel, and players whose point of entry is the games. Each group is waiting for something slightly different.

That is an opportunity—and a communications challenge. A franchise can be active in multiple formats while its most visible adaptation remains between seasons. It also means headlines that simply say “Solo Leveling returns” are no longer specific enough. Which storyline? Which medium? Which territory? Which date? Those questions should be part of the headline, not afterthoughts.

For webtoon creators, this restart offers another useful observation. Even a gigantic brand relies on individual production teams whose members can change, pause, and need time to rebuild a schedule. The audience sees a new cover and a date; behind it is a transition of creative responsibility. The best outcome is not merely the fastest comeback. It is a comeback that gives its new artist room to deliver consistent work.

RogueVerse verdict: a welcome comeback, with one necessary correction

The genuinely good news is that Solo Leveling: Ragnarok has a date again. Thursday, November 5 brings three Korean webtoon installments—Episodes 69 through 71—alongside the arrival of a new illustrator and the beginning of Season 3. That is a real milestone after the January hiatus, and it is worth celebrating.

The correction is equally important: anime fans are not receiving three new television episodes on that date. The animated franchise's next publicly announced project is Beyond the System, while the timing of a future third television season remains separate.

For readers, the best preparation is simple: revisit the ending of Ragnarok Season 2, check your preferred official platform's update status, and approach the new artwork with an open mind. This is a return for Suho's story—and a chance for that story to establish an identity that is more than the shadow of its famous predecessor.
