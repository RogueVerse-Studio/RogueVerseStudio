Did Ned Just Break Doctor Strange's Spell?

Spider-Man: Brand New Day may have hidden the answer years earlier in No Way Home.

ROGUEVERSE MOVIES / THEORY CHECK • AUGUST 8, 2026

MAJOR SPOILER WARNING: This article discusses the ending of Spider-Man: Brand New Day and Spider-Man: No Way Home.

The final handshake in Spider-Man: Brand New Day lasts only moments, but it may have created the MCU's newest magical mystery. Peter Parker meets Ned Leeds again in a world that no longer remembers Peter. Peter reaches out. Ned responds. Then their old best-friend handshake, a gesture they performed long before Doctor Strange erased Peter from the world's memory, seems to return automatically.

Then Ned reacts.

That is the moment fans have seized upon. Did Ned merely recognize a strangely familiar movement? Did his body retain something his conscious memory lost? Or did Peter and Ned accidentally create a magical anchor years ago, one capable of reaching through Strange's spell?

RogueVerse's answer is deliberately less sensational than the viral version: the handshake being a literal secret spell is unproven, but the idea that it functions as an anchor between Ned's magical aptitude, procedural memory and his buried relationship with Peter is surprisingly plausible.

What the Movie Actually Establishes

Sony's official synopsis makes Peter's condition explicit: he is fighting crime in a world that does not remember him while watching his old friends move on. That distinction matters. The story is not beginning from a world where Ned secretly remembers everything. Peter's isolation is real.

Near the end, however, Peter and Ned encounter one another and perform their signature handshake. Jacob Batalon later told People that performing the gesture with Tom Holland came back "like muscle memory." That comment is about the actors' experience making the scene, not an official explanation of the MCU's magic, but the wording fits the scene almost perfectly.

The body remembers something the conscious mind supposedly cannot.

That gives the theory a stronger foundation than simply saying friendship beats magic. The movie has selected a physical ritual shared by these two characters and placed it at the exact point where Ned appears to experience recognition.

The Viral Theory: What If the Handshake Was Already Magical?

The wilder version goes back to No Way Home. Ned discovers that he can operate a Sling Ring and open portals with almost no formal training. His family history is played for comedy, but the film undeniably establishes that Ned has unusual access to sorcery.

Fans then noticed a visual spark associated with Ned, Peter, the handshake and the Sling Ring. The theory argues that the gesture may have been unintentionally touched by magic. If so, repeating the handshake in Brand New Day would not simply be nostalgia. It would be reactivating an old magical connection created before Strange's final spell.

GamesRadar highlighted this interpretation after it spread through Marvel fan communities in early August. It is clever because it connects three separate pieces of continuity: Ned's magical aptitude, the handshake's history and the ending of Brand New Day.

But clever is not confirmed.

The Biggest Problem With the Literal Spell Theory

There is no official statement from Sony, Marvel, director Destin Daniel Cretton, the screenwriters, Holland or Batalon confirming that Peter and Ned accidentally enchanted their handshake. The visual spark can be interpreted as Sling Ring activity rather than the birth of an independent spell.

There is also a power-scaling problem. Ned being able to use a Sling Ring does not automatically mean he can override one of Doctor Strange's most consequential spells. If basic magical aptitude made someone resistant, the MCU would have to explain why trained sorcerers were not obviously exempt.

So the strongest version of this theory is not "Ned secretly cast a better spell than Doctor Strange." That requires too many assumptions.

The stronger version is stranger and more emotional.

Maybe Strange Erased Explicit Memory, Not Every Pathway

Human memory is not one single system. Knowing a person's identity and automatically performing a practiced physical sequence are not the same kind of remembering. That gives Brand New Day a useful storytelling loophole even before magic enters the discussion.

Ned may be unable to consciously retrieve "Peter Parker was my best friend," while still retaining a procedural trace of something he performed hundreds of times with Peter. The handshake becomes evidence that Strange's spell changed access to Peter rather than perfectly rewriting every consequence of Peter's existence.

Imagine a library where the books remain on the shelves but every catalog entry for one author has vanished. The stories still exist. You simply cannot search for them by the missing name.

The handshake could be the equivalent of finding one of those books by touch.

Why Ned Is the Perfect Person to Find the Crack

Ned has three advantages most people do not. First, his friendship with Peter predates the spell by years. Second, the handshake is a deeply practiced physical ritual tied specifically to that friendship. Third, Ned has already demonstrated an affinity for MCU sorcery.

None of those facts alone defeats Strange's magic. Together, however, they make Ned an unusually good candidate for experiencing a contradiction the spell was never designed to handle cleanly.

His mind says: I do not know this person.

His body says: I know exactly what comes next.

If his latent magical sensitivity lets him perceive that contradiction more sharply than an ordinary person, the handshake does not need to be a spell itself. It only needs to become the trigger that forces two incompatible realities together.

And That Changes the Meaning of "Peter"

The ending is deliberately ambiguous enough to support multiple readings. One interpretation is that Ned genuinely remembers Peter. Another is that the handshake helps him realize the stranger standing in front of him is connected to Spider-Man, whose history was not erased in the same way. A third is that Ned experiences only an emotional flash, the first crack rather than the full restoration.

That uncertainty is important. If Marvel wanted to restore Ned completely with no complication, the movie could simply show a montage of recovered memories. Instead, it cuts at the threshold.

The question becomes the hook.

Could Jean Grey Be Involved Instead?

Brand New Day also introduces another variable: Jean Grey and telepathy. Some viewers have proposed that psychic interference during the movie destabilizes the boundaries Strange placed around Peter's identity. That explanation would account for memory anomalies without requiring Ned to have accidentally created a spell years earlier.

It also gives Marvel a convenient mechanism for exploring memory without declaring Strange's spell weak. A powerful telepath interacting with minds already altered by magic could reveal what remains underneath rather than simply overpowering the enchantment.

This possibility cannot currently be ruled out. In fact, it may coexist with the handshake theory. Jean could weaken the wall; the handshake could be what Ned finds behind it.

What About MJ?

MJ is the most important counterexample. If emotional connection alone can restore memory, why does Peter's relationship with MJ not instantly defeat the spell?

That is precisely why Ned's magical history matters to the theory. Peter and MJ's bond may be deeper romantically, but Ned has the combination of emotional history, practiced physical ritual and demonstrated sorcery.

Alternatively, MJ may retain her own fragments without recognizing their source. The MCU can explore familiarity, emotional residue and explicit autobiographical memory as different layers. That would preserve the cost of No Way Home instead of flipping everyone's memories back on like a light switch.

The Storytelling Reason This Works

The best Marvel theories do more than solve continuity puzzles. They create better character drama.

If Ned suddenly remembers every detail because a spell randomly expires, Peter gets his friend back with very little emotional cost. If Ned has to reconstruct Peter through physical memory, magical sensitivity and fragments of emotion, then their friendship becomes part of the solution.

That is far more appropriate for a movie built around Peter discovering whether Peter Parker can still exist alongside Spider-Man.

The handshake represents Peter Parker, not the mask. It belongs to two friends. It is mundane, goofy, intimate and completely useless in a superhero battle. That is exactly why it makes such an effective key.

RogueVerse Theory Check

Literal "Peter and Ned accidentally created a handshake spell": 5/10. Fun, possible, but unsupported by direct confirmation and dependent on interpreting the Sling Ring spark very specifically.

Handshake as a magical/procedural memory anchor: 8/10. This requires fewer assumptions and connects directly to what Brand New Day shows us.

Overall theory plausibility: 7.5/10.

The strongest interpretation is therefore not that two teenagers accidentally invented a counterspell powerful enough to embarrass Doctor Strange.

The handshake may be an anchor. Ned's body remembers what his conscious mind cannot, and his magical sensitivity may allow that contradiction to become the first real crack in Strange's spell.

If Ned fully remembers Peter in the next chapter, No Way Home may have planted the mechanism years before anyone knew the handshake would matter.

And if he does not fully remember? The scene still tells us something potentially enormous: Strange's magic may control the name and the conscious memory, but it may not be able to erase every imprint a person leaves behind.

That would mean Peter Parker is not gone from the people who loved him.

He is buried.

And Ned may have just found the first door.
